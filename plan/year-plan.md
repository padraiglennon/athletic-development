# The year

For a squad of 75 to 80 U11 boys training at 5.30 on Tuesday and Thursday.

One theme every four sessions, taken from the [activity bank](activities.md). Four sessions is two Tuesdays and two Thursdays, so a theme gets two hurling nights and two football nights. There is no need for anything more complicated at this age, and anything more complicated will not survive contact with a wet Thursday in March.

## Where the boys are

Nine to eleven years old, nearly all of them still before their growth spurt. The GAA framework puts them at the very start of everything, and says the job at this age is basic movement skills. Not fitness, not strength.

So the year has one aim: **by next September, these boys can start, stop, turn, sprint and land properly, and they know seven body shapes by name.**

That is a low bar to describe and a hard bar to actually clear with 80 boys.

## The fortnight

There is a match every Saturday, hurling one week and football the next, so the rhythm runs in two week blocks. Each code gets two nights in the fortnight, one either side of its own match.

| | Tuesday | Thursday | Saturday |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Hurling. Teach the theme, slowly, then push it | Football. Use it at match pace, then ease off | Football match |
| Week 2 | Football. Teach the theme, slowly, then push it | Hurling. Use it at match pace, then ease off | Hurling match |

Thursday is the code that is on that Saturday, in match format. Tuesday is the code that played the Saturday just gone.

So for any one code the run is Thursday at match pace, the match on Saturday, then the following Tuesday to teach it properly again. That Tuesday is four days clear of the next match, which is why it is the night to push. No Thursday ever is.

Nothing new is ever introduced on a Thursday. Two days before a match is not the time for a boy to meet a movement for the first time.

## Rules that do not change

1. Nothing heavier than the boy himself. No weights, no bands, no vests, ever.
2. If it looks bad, make it easier. Slower and correct beats fast and messy.
3. No fitness work at our station. The match rotation does that, twice a week. The endurance theme is not an exception: it teaches pacing, not fitness, and nobody is ever run into the ground.
4. Everyone does the same programme. No boy is ever told he is in the bottom group.
5. Ease off every Thursday. There is a match two days later, every week.
6. If they are not enjoying it, it has not worked, no matter what was on the plan.
7. Mixture of ball use in each session (from session 12 onwards). Each session starts with a focus on form or the movement goal without the ball (minutes 1 to 6), but the second exercise (minutes 6 to 11) includes the ball where possible. Earlier sessions (1 to 11) stay untouched.

## The order of the themes

A theme runs for four sessions, which is two weeks of Tuesdays and Thursdays. Count sessions, not weeks. A night lost to a storm or a clashing fixture should cost the theme one night, not push a whole theme off the calendar, so when a night goes the theme just runs a few days longer and everything after it slides.

Four sessions is exactly two turns of the fixture cycle, so every theme gets two hurling nights and two football nights. That is not a coincidence, it falls straight out of a match every Saturday.

| Sessions | Dates, if nothing is cancelled | Theme | Why here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 4 | 25 Aug to 3 Sep 2026 | 1. Starting | Easy to run, instant, gets them used to the station |
| 5 to 8 | 8 to 17 Sep 2026 | 2. Endurance | Teaches one pace. Goes early while the evenings are bright enough for a big loop |
| 9 to 12 | 22 Sep to 1 Oct 2026 | 3. Sprints | Slow running and fast running side by side |
| 13 to 16 | 6 to 15 Oct 2026 | 4. Stopping | The one that matters most. A boy who can now sprint has to learn to brake |
| 17 to 20 | 20 to 29 Oct 2026 | 5. Jumping | Works in a small space as the evenings go dark |
| 21 onwards | 3 Nov 2026 onwards | Not planned yet | See below |


**From session 21 onwards this page is out of date.** The move to four session themes was agreed after the autumn was already written, so only the twenty nights to Halloween have been reworked. **November is the body shapes theme.** The shapes used to be five minutes of every night. They are now a theme of their own and they run first after Halloween. The seven shapes are in the [activity bank](activities.md).

The remaining themes in the [activity bank](activities.md) are turning, sidestepping, backwards and sideways, doing it with a hurl or a ball, joining it up, and reacting. At four sessions each that is another twenty four nights, which does not fill the year on its own, so most themes will need a second run. Deciding the order of that is the next job.

Stopping and jumping are the two that reduce injuries. If the year falls apart and you only get a few themes done properly, do those two, and do them again.

The session by session plan for the five autumn themes is in [autumn-2026.md](autumn-2026.md).

## December

Nothing organised from us. Push the boys towards a different sport for a few weeks, and leave them alone otherwise.

This is not laziness, it is in the GAA document. It asks coaches to encourage other sports and to protect time for messing about. A boy who plays basketball in December comes back a better mover in January.

The season runs January to January, so this is the only break in the calendar. That makes it more important, not less. If matches carry on through December, the break is from us, not from the club, and it is worth agreeing with the other coaches what the boys are actually being asked to do that month.

## Two sessions and a match

Two training nights and a match every Saturday is a decent amount for a ten year old, especially for the boys who also play soccer or basketball or swim on top of it. There is no free weekend to soak up a heavy week, so the easing off on a Thursday is not optional.

The GAA document asks for two things here, and both are easy enough to do:

- Do not let a boy do something hard three days running. That mostly means watching the boy who has a school match on a Friday and a club match on the Saturday.
- Ring the coaches of the other sports the boys play. One phone call a month between the two of you sorts out most of it. Nobody needs a spreadsheet.

## What we are not doing

**No testing and no measuring.** No timed runs, no bleep test, no height and weight records. The endurance theme counts laps as a group and never as an individual, and the sprints theme has no stopwatch anywhere in it. The GAA document does recommend measuring height and weight twice a year for safety, and we have decided against it for this squad. At U11 that is a reasonable call. It is worth revisiting at U13 and U14, when boys start growing at very different rates and the risk of injury during a growth spurt becomes a real thing to manage.

**No individual records.** Nobody is tracked, nobody is graded, nobody is compared.

**No selection.** The ability grouping exists so the coaching can be pitched right. It is not a ranking and it should never be described as one.
