# The year

Written on 24 August 2026, revised on 25 August, for a squad of 75 to 80 U11 boys training at 5.30 on Tuesday and Thursday.

One theme every eight sessions, ten themes, taken from the [activity bank](activities.md). Eight goes at it is enough for it to stick and short enough that nobody gets sick of it. There is no need for anything more complicated at this age, and anything more complicated will not survive contact with a wet Thursday in March.

## Where the boys are

Nine to eleven years old, nearly all of them still before their growth spurt. The GAA framework puts them at the very start of everything, and says the job at this age is basic movement skills. Not fitness, not strength.

So the year has one aim: **by next September, these boys can start, stop, turn and land properly, and they know seven body shapes by name.**

That is a low bar to describe and a hard bar to actually clear with 80 boys.

## The fortnight

There is a match every Saturday, hurling one week and football the next, so the rhythm runs in two week blocks. Each code gets two nights in the fortnight, one either side of its own match.

| | Tuesday | Thursday | Saturday |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Hurling. Teach the theme, slowly, then push it | Football. Use it at match pace, then ease off | Football match |
| Week 2 | Football. Teach the theme, slowly, then push it | Hurling. Use it at match pace, then ease off | Hurling match |

Thursday is the code that is on that Saturday, in match format. Tuesday is the code that played the Saturday just gone.

So for any one code the run is Thursday at match pace, the match on Saturday, then the following Tuesday to teach it properly again. That Tuesday is four days clear of the next match, which is why it is the night to push. No Thursday ever is.

Nothing new is ever introduced on a Thursday. Two days before a match is not the time for a boy to meet a movement for the first time.

## Rules that do not change

1. Nothing heavier than the boy himself. No weights, no bands, no vests, ever.
2. If it looks bad, make it easier. Slower and correct beats fast and messy.
3. No fitness work at our station. The match rotation does that, twice a week.
4. Everyone does the same programme. No boy is ever told he is in the bottom group.
5. Ease off every Thursday. There is a match two days later, every week.
6. If they are not enjoying it, it has not worked, no matter what was on the plan.

## The order of the themes

A theme runs for eight sessions, which is four weeks of Tuesdays and Thursdays. Count sessions, not weeks. A night lost to a storm or a clashing fixture should cost the theme one night, not push a whole theme off the calendar, so when a night goes the theme just runs a few days longer and everything after it slides.

Eight sessions is also exactly two turns of the fixture cycle, so every theme gets four hurling nights and four football nights. That is not a coincidence, it falls straight out of a match every Saturday.

| Sessions | Dates, if nothing is cancelled | Theme | Why here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 8 | 25 Aug to 17 Sep 2026 | 1. Starting | Easy to run, instant, gets them used to the station |
| 9 to 16 | 22 Sep to 15 Oct 2026 | 2. Stopping | The one that matters most, so do not leave it until November |
| 17 to 24 | 20 Oct to 12 Nov 2026 | 3. Landing | Works in a small space as the evenings go dark |
| 25 to 28 | 17 to 26 Nov 2026 | Back over the first three | Four odd nights before the break. Do not start a theme you cannot finish |
| | December 2026 | Break | Nothing organised. Let them play something else |
| 29 to 36 | 5 to 28 Jan 2027 | 4. Turning | Back after the break, so a familiar shape of night with new content |
| 37 to 44 | 2 to 25 Feb 2027 | 5. Running tall | Needs calm and repetition, so before the season gets busy |
| 45 to 52 | 2 to 25 Mar 2027 | 6. Sidestepping | Season under way, they will want something game-like |
| 53 to 60 | 30 Mar to 22 Apr 2027 | 7. Backwards and sideways | The gap in most boys' movement |
| 61 to 68 | 27 Apr to 20 May 2027 | 8. With a hurl or a ball | Ties the station to the skills station |
| 69 to 76 | 25 May to 17 Jun 2027 | 9. Joining it up | Everything from the year, chained together |
| 77 to 84 | 22 Jun to 15 Jul 2027 | 10. Reacting | Finish at the closest thing to a match |

Ten themes at eight sessions each is eighty four nights including the November four, which lands in mid July. The weeks between then and September are the slack. They will be eaten by cancellations, holidays and the county final nobody planned for, and whatever is left goes back over the themes that did not land.

Themes 2, 3 and 4 are the ones that reduce injuries. If the year falls apart and you only get three themes done properly, do those.

The session by session plan for the first three themes is in [autumn-2026.md](autumn-2026.md).

## December

Nothing organised from us. Push the boys towards a different sport for a few weeks, and leave them alone otherwise.

This is not laziness, it is in the GAA document. It asks coaches to encourage other sports and to protect time for messing about. A boy who plays basketball in December comes back a better mover in January.

## Two sessions and a match

Two training nights and a match every Saturday is a decent amount for a ten year old, especially for the boys who also play soccer or basketball or swim on top of it. There is no free weekend to soak up a heavy week, so the easing off on a Thursday is not optional.

The GAA document asks for two things here, and both are easy enough to do:

- Do not let a boy do something hard three days running. That mostly means watching the boy who has a school match on a Friday and a club match on the Saturday.
- Ring the coaches of the other sports the boys play. One phone call a month between the two of you sorts out most of it. Nobody needs a spreadsheet.

## What we are not doing

**No testing and no measuring.** No timed runs, no bleep test, no height and weight records. The GAA document does recommend measuring height and weight twice a year for safety, and we have decided against it for this squad. At U11 that is a reasonable call. It is worth revisiting at U13 and U14, when boys start growing at very different rates and the risk of injury during a growth spurt becomes a real thing to manage.

**No individual records.** Nobody is tracked, nobody is graded, nobody is compared.

**No selection.** The ability grouping exists so the coaching can be pitched right. It is not a ranking and it should never be described as one.
