The hardest night of the block. This is where the pace goes up, because there is nowhere else in the fortnight to put it.

## On the ground

- Twelve cones, six lanes, 15 metres long
- 25 marker discs beside the lanes, one per boy
- No balls tonight, hands free

![Six lanes 15 metres long, a cone at each end, and a marker disc for each boy beside the lanes](../diagrams/lanes-15m.svg)

Set it up before the first group arrives and leave it for all three.

## Splitting them up

- Four boys to a lane, one behind the other behind the start cone. Spare boys go to the shortest lane.
- The boy at the front of each lane runs. Six lanes, so six boys go together on your whistle. That is what a wave means below.
- After his run he jogs back up the outside of his lane and joins the back of it. He is up again in about twenty seconds.

## Structure and rules

**0 to 2, wake up game: sprint to the nearest cone.**

They jog about inside the grid, hands empty. On "cone", every boy sprints to the nearest of the twelve cones and stops dead beside it, then back to jogging. Four or five goes.

**2 to 8, movement: starting on a shout.**

Every start so far has gone on a whistle they could see coming. Tonight they cannot.

*On a shout.* They set up in a standing or crouch start. You wait somewhere between one and five seconds, then shout "go". Vary the wait, and stand behind them so they cannot read you.

*With eyes shut.* The same again, eyes closed until the shout, then open and run.

Four waves of each. If a boy stumbles or falls out of the start, put him back to plain standing starts for the rest of the night.

**8 to 13, body shape: the sit.**

The sit on one leg. One foot on his disc, the other leg held out in front, a hand on a partner's shoulder for balance. He bends the standing knee as far as he can and comes back up.

Five each leg, slow. Most of them will only get part of the way down, which is fine.

**13 to 17, challenge game: race off the ground.**

Pairs of roughly the same size, one pair per lane. Both lie flat on their fronts on the start line, a metre apart, hands under their shoulders, facing down the lane. On the whistle they get up and race ten metres.

Six or seven races, new partner each time. Nothing written down.

Rules, the same every night:

- Nobody starts before the whistle, and one coach whistles for all six lanes.
- A boy who has run jogs back up the outside of his lane.
- Every boy stays on his own disc for the shapes.
- Same station for all three groups. Set it up once, run it three times.

## Progression

Work down this and stop wherever the group is. Getting to the bottom is not the aim.

1. All six starts, one wave at each, to settle them in.
2. Standing and crouch starts on a shout, with the wait varied. Four goes.
3. The same with eyes shut until the shout. Four goes.

## Coaching points

- **Coach one thing tonight: reacting without falling out of the start.** If a boy is falling over, back to standing starts. No argument.
- Eyes shut only works if it is quiet. One voice.
- The pace goes up tonight. It never goes up on a Thursday.

## If it is not working

- **Running late.** Cut the wake up game. Never cut the body shapes.
- **More than four waiting in a lane.** Add a seventh lane. Boys in a queue learn nothing.
- **It looks bad.** Go back a step and slow it down.
- **Lashing rain.** The lying race becomes a crouch race. Eyes shut is fine in the wet, they are only running straight.
