The hardest night of the block, in the theme that matters most all year. Push it, and be strict about what you let past.

## On the ground

- Twelve cones: six lanes with the stop line at 10 metres
- 25 marker discs beside the lanes, one per boy
- No balls tonight, hands free

![Six lanes with a stop line at 10 metres and five metres beyond it to run off, and a marker disc for each boy beside the lanes](../diagrams/stop-line.svg)

Set it up before the first group arrives and leave it for all three.

## Splitting them up

- Four boys to a lane, one behind the other behind the start cone. Spare boys go to the shortest lane.
- The boy at the front of each lane runs. Six lanes, so six boys go together on your whistle. That is what a wave means below.
- The cone in the middle of the lane is the stop line. The five metres past it is room to run off if he cannot stop, not somewhere to keep running.
- After his go he jogs back up the outside of his lane and joins the back of it.

## Structure and rules

**0 to 2, wake up game: traffic lights.**

They run about inside the grid. "Green" is run, "red" is stop dead and hold still. Anyone still moving after red does five sits on the spot. Six or seven goes, and make some of the greens short.

**2 to 8, movement: stopping on one foot.**

Everything so far has been stopping on two feet. In a match it is usually one, and that is where knees get hurt, so this is done slowly and carefully.

He runs in and stops on one foot only, the other foot off the ground, and holds still for three seconds. Knee bent, knee pointing out over the toes, hips level.

Walk it, then jog it, then run it, and a boy only moves up a step when the one before it looks right. Nobody moves up because his friends did.

**8 to 13, body shape: the bow.**

The bow on one leg with arms straight out sideways, like wings. Ten seconds each leg.

The arms make balancing harder and they show you instantly whose hips are dropping.

**13 to 17, challenge game: stop on the number.**

As each wave runs in, hold up a number of fingers where they can see you. They stop on the line and shout the number back.

If nobody gets it, they were watching their feet. Six or seven waves.

Rules, the same every night:

- Nobody starts before the whistle, and one coach whistles for all six lanes.
- A boy who has run jogs back up the outside of his lane.
- Every boy stays on his own disc for the shapes.
- Same station for all three groups. Set it up once, run it three times.

## Progression

Work down this and stop wherever the group is. Getting to the bottom is not the aim.

1. Walk in, stop on one foot, hold three seconds. Five each foot.
2. Jog in, stop on one foot. Four each foot.
3. Run in, stop on one foot. Only the boys whose knees stayed out at step two.

## Coaching points

- **Coach one thing tonight: knees falling inwards on the one foot stop.** If it looks bad, back to two feet. No argument, and no explanation needed beyond "we will come back to it".
- Slower and correct beats faster and messy, and tonight is the night to say that out loud.
- This is the most useful fifteen minutes in the whole year. Treat it that way.

## If it is not working

- **Running late.** Cut the wake up game. Never cut the body shapes.
- **More than four waiting in a lane.** Add a seventh lane. Boys in a queue learn nothing.
- **It looks bad.** Go back a step and slow it down.
- **Lashing rain.** Do the whole thing at walking and jogging pace. One foot stops on wet grass at full speed are how boys get hurt.
