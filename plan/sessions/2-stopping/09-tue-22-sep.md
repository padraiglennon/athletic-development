# Session 9, Tuesday 22 September

New theme, so tonight is a teaching night before it is a pushing one. Most injuries at this age come out of a bad stop, and nobody ever teaches it.

## On the ground

- Twelve cones: six lanes with the stop line at 10 metres
- 25 marker discs beside the lanes, one per boy
- A hurl each

![Six lanes with a stop line at 10 metres and five metres beyond it to run off, and a marker disc for each boy beside the lanes](../diagrams/stop-line.svg)

Set it up before the first group arrives and leave it for all three.

## Splitting them up

- Four boys to a lane, one behind the other behind the start cone. Spare boys go to the shortest lane.
- The boy at the front of each lane runs. Six lanes, so six boys go together on your whistle. That is what a wave means below.
- The cone in the middle of the lane is the stop line. The five metres past it is room to run off if he cannot stop, not somewhere to keep running.
- After his go he jogs back up the outside of his lane and joins the back of it.

## Structure and rules

**0 to 2, wake up game: traffic lights.**

They run about inside the grid. "Green" is run, "red" is stop dead and hold still. Anyone still moving after red does five sits on the spot. Six or seven goes, and make some of the greens short.

**2 to 8, movement: two steps to stop.**

The rule all block is two steps to stop, no more, and no falling forward over the line.

He runs at the line, and the last two steps sink down: knees bend, backside goes back, chest stays up, and he holds still. Say out loud that this is the sit they have been holding for eight nights, done while moving. That connection is the whole reason the sit came first.

Walk it first, six waves. Then jog it, six waves. Nobody runs at it tonight.

**8 to 13, body shape: the bow.**

Every boy on his own disc, an arm's length apart. The bow is the second of the seven shapes and it is the shape a stop happens in.

Say these out loud as they do it:

1. "Stand tall, feet under your hips, knees soft."
2. "Push your backside back behind you, like you are closing a door with it."
3. "Let your chest come forward and keep your back flat like a table."
4. "Slide your hands down your legs, and stop when your back stops being flat."
5. "Hold. One, two, three, four, five."
6. "Stand up by pushing your hips forward."

Ten of them. A hurl held along his back is the quick check: it should touch his backside, his back and the back of his head all at once.

**13 to 17, challenge game: stop on the number.**

As each wave runs in, hold up a number of fingers where they can see you. They stop on the line and shout the number back.

If nobody gets it, they were watching their feet. Six or seven waves.

Rules, the same every night:

- Nobody starts before the whistle, and one coach whistles for all six lanes.
- A boy who has run jogs back up the outside of his lane.
- Every boy stays on his own disc for the shapes.
- Same station for all three groups. Set it up once, run it three times.

## Progression

Work down this and stop wherever the group is. Getting to the bottom is not the aim.

1. Walk in and stop on the line in two steps. Six goes.
2. Jog in and stop in two steps. Six goes.
3. Jog in, stop, hold the bow for two seconds, then walk back. Six goes.

## Coaching points

- **Coach one thing tonight: straight legs at the stop.** If a boy stops upright and stiff, stop the lane and show it again. It should look like sitting down into the stop.
- Count the steps out loud with them. Two, not five.
- Teaching night. Slow and correct first, pace later in the block.

## If it is not working

- **Running late.** Cut the wake up game. Never cut the body shapes.
- **More than four waiting in a lane.** Add a seventh lane. Boys in a queue learn nothing.
- **It looks bad.** Go back a step and slow it down.
- **Lashing rain.** Wet grass makes them skid. Move the stop line in a metre or two and keep it at walking and jogging pace.
