# Sample exercises from the club sports director

Source: `sample-exercises-barry.pdf`, four pages, sent by Barry, the club sports director.

Page one is a game card from the LTAD Network (copyright 2021, www.ltadnetwork.com), so it is somebody else's material and worth crediting if we pass it around. Pages two to four are screenshots of drills, football only. No age group is written on any of them, and Barry has confirmed the pack is meant for every age group in the club.

## What is in it

**Page 1, the gauntlet.** An attacker has to get from a start line to a finish line without being tagged. Defenders stand on lines across the channel and can only move sideways along their own line, using lateral shuffles, crossovers and directional steps. The card lists the movements it is training: linear acceleration, deceleration, cutting and curved running for the attacker, lateral shuffle and crossover for the defenders. It suggests four ways to change it, by space, task, equipment and number of people.

**Page 2, the 20 metre shuttle with kick passes.** Three players. The middle one works between two cones 20 metres apart for 30 seconds, kick passing to one end, taking the return off the other, and back again. Repeated with a high catch and a low catch.

**Page 3, the five player kick pass and run.** Groups of five over 40 metres. One kick passes over a player's head to a third, then takes that player's position. The middle player waits for the catch, times a run and solos to the end. And so on around the group.

**Page 4, tempo running.** A pitch diagram. Jog a length, tempo run a length, walk, jog, tempo run, walk, going round the pitch continuously.

## What we are taking

**The gauntlet, and it is a good addition.** It is the one item in the pack that genuinely does work at every age, because the card comes with four ways to change it, by space, task, equipment and number of people. Turn all four down and it fits our U11s. Turn them up and it would stretch a minor squad. That is what a drill for all age groups actually looks like.

It also solves a problem the plan already has, which is that sidestepping and lateral movement are hard to drill without half the group standing watching.

The reason it works with 25 boys is that the defenders are not resting. Shuffling sideways along a line is the exact movement we want out of theme 7, so while four boys are defending they are getting the session, not queuing for it. Three channels side by side, eight boys in each, swap the defenders and the attackers halfway.

It is written up in the [activity bank](../plan/activities.md#the-gauntlet) and it replaces the challenge game in themes 6, 7 and 10.

## What we are not taking, and why

Since the pack is meant for everyone, this is not a mismatch, it is a judgement call about our squad and our seventeen minutes.

Worth saying first that the action statement deliberately puts no ages on anything. It says the stage a boy is at depends on the boy, and it leaves the judging to the coach standing in front of him. So a pack aimed at all age groups is a fair thing to send round. It just means somebody has to do that judging, and for the U11s that is us.

**The two kick passing drills.** Nothing wrong with either at U11. The question is which station they belong to, and it is not ours. Both are skills drills with conditioning running attached, and our seventeen minutes do neither of those jobs. The match rotation is the fitness and the skills rotation is the kick passing. Hand both to the skills coaches and they are a good addition over there.

The size problem does not go away at any age either. Page three needs groups of five and 40 metres, which with 25 boys is five groups strung across most of a pitch, and it takes about a minute to explain against our thirty second rule. That is four of the seventeen minutes gone before anyone has moved.

**The tempo running.** This is the one where all age groups and the framework pull against each other, so it is worth being straight about it.

The action statement's recommended way to build endurance is playing, not running. Small games, smaller pitch, fewer players, with Irish research behind it in hurling. It says in as many words that basic movement skills are the job at this age and fitness is not. Our boys already get a match rotation twice a week and a match every Saturday, so that box is ticked before we start, and laps of the pitch would only add running volume to a nine year old who has no use for it. See [the notes on the action statement](action-statement-notes.md).

It is a normal method for a minor or adult squad in pre-season. At U11 it is the one item in the pack to decline outright rather than move to another station.

## Worth going back to Barry about

- The real question is not age, it is which station. Say yes to both kick passing drills for the skills coaches and most of the disagreement disappears.
- The tempo running is the only outright no, and only for the U11s. It is a reasonable session further up the club.
- Ask for more of the LTAD Network cards. They come as a series and the gauntlet one is genuinely well made. A card that carries its own adaptations is the format that survives being sent to every age group at once.
